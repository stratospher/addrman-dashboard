Relay health

Address-relay volume, packing and rate limiting over time, from a node's own debug.log. Parsed in this page — the file never leaves your machine.

Open a grepped debug.log

Drag a file here, or

# both line types into one file — needs debug=net on the node
zstdcat -f ~/.bitcoin/debug.log*.zst ~/.bitcoin/debug.log 2>/dev/null \
  | grep -E 'Received addr:|sending getaddr' | sort > relay.txt

The sending getaddr lines are optional but let solicited replies be told apart from unsolicited bulk pushes. .gz works; for .zst decompress first.

Parsing…

Volume

Addresses received

The headline total. Volume alone does not tell you much — a surge made of re-announcements of addresses you already hold updates nTime in place and inserts nothing, so it costs no eviction. Read this against packing below: address volume is not addrman pressure, address novelty is.

Addresses received, split by channel

The ≤10 band is the only one Core will relay onward (net_processing.cpp: nTime > now−10min && !m_getaddr_sent && vAddr.size() <= 10 && IsRoutable), so it is the amplified channel. 11–99 is a peer flushing a backed-up relay queue — a second-order gauge of how much relayable traffic the rest of the network is carrying. ≥100 is bulk, solicited or not.

Packing — addresses per message

Rate limiting

Intensity — addresses dropped per 10,000 received

The bucket refills at MAX_ADDR_RATE_PER_SECOND = 0.1/s — a budget of 8,640 addresses per peer per day — capped at 1,000, and starts at 1.0. Sending a GETADDR grants the peer +1,000, so a reply is never limited but an unsolicited dump to a fresh connection is almost entirely dropped. This is a node-side measurement: it records what you decided to drop, so unlike anything derived from address timestamps it cannot be shaped by what peers claim.

Concentration — worst peer's share of the drops

High share means one peer is over the bucket on its own. A collapse to a few percent means no single peer exceeds the budget any more — the drops are the ordinary background of many peers each just grazing it.

Unsolicited bulk

Bulk pushes we never asked for

A message of ≥100 addresses from a peer we sent no GETADDR to. Because the token bucket starts at 1.0 and only refills at 0.1/s, such a push lands on a nearly empty bucket and is ~99% dropped — so it contributes almost nothing to address volume and almost everything to the rate-limit counter. It is invisible in the volume charts above and loud here.

Peers

Distinct peers sending addresses

Peer ids are per-connection and restart at 0 when the node does, so a bucket spanning a restart double-counts, and per-peer totals across one are meaningless. Resolve ids to addresses via the receive version message lines before reasoning about who anything was.
